# Build one quiet frame, three loud worlds

Top slot providers win on a **shared, nearly invisible player shell plus a strict cause-and-effect motion grammar**, and they let each game's identity live in the frame, symbols, palette, sound and feature art rather than in the controls. The clearest proof is Pragmatic Play: the same five-toggle settings menu (Quick Spin, Battery Saver, Ambient Music, Sound FX, Intro Screen) appears in a 2019-era 5-reel lines game and in the 6x5 pays-anywhere Gates of Olympus ([Hot Safari](https://free-slots-no-download.com/pragmatic-play/10450-hot-safari/); [Clash of Slots](https://clashofslots.com/slots/pragmatic-play/gates-of-olympus/)). Regulation now shapes that shell as much as design does: Great Britain and Ontario require **2.5 s per spin, no turbo, quick spin or slam stop, no autoplay, and no win effects when the return is at or below the stake** ([UKGC RTS 14](https://www.gamblingcommission.gov.uk/standards/remote-gambling-and-software-technical-standards/rts-14-responsible-product-design); [AGCO](https://agco.ca/en/book/export/html/245361)), so every speed and celebration rule has to be a market switch. Under the hood, hiring and documentation point to **PixiJS/WebGL plus TypeScript inside an in-house framework, with Spine for skeletal animation** ([Play'n GO ad](https://webbjobb.io/jobb/234321/front-end-game-architect); [Hacksaw ad](https://freehire.me/jobs/game-developer-frontend-hacksaw-studios-tgbkzd4j); [Esoteric](https://esotericsoftware.com/blog/spine-pixi-v8-runtime-released)). For Lumina Games, the recommendation is to extract its existing Vault 20K deck, ticker and notification layer into an original "Lumina Shell", drive all motion from one timing table with market-gated turbo/skip/celebration columns, keep the no-idle-motion and no-shake rules as a differentiator, and decide on PixiJS for the two new games only after measured spikes. The evidence has a hard limit, and every reader should know it before acting: **provider demo hosts were blocked (HTTP 403) for the entire research pass, so this report contains zero MEASURED provider values**. Every timing below is either a third-party reference spec or a Lumina recommendation, and Phase 0 of the plan exists to replace them with real measurements. Lumina is pre-launch; nothing here implies it is certified or licensed, and the regulatory material is not legal advice.

**How to read the labels.** VERIFIED means a fetched source states it. MEASURED means measured with a stated method; there are no MEASURED provider values in this report. INFERRED means the researchers' or this writer's reasoning, or unsourced industry recall. REFERENCE-SPEC means a number from an unnamed third-party draft presentation spec ([Magic Vault 5x3 presentation flow, "DEV ONLY"](https://math-gdd-dev.chowchowhome.duckdns.org/games/magic-vault-5x3/gdd/magic-vault-5x3-presentation-flow)); it shows how a studio writes timings down, but **it is not Pragmatic's or any named provider's data**. REC means a Lumina recommendation made in this report.

## 1. Fifteen shared practices define the modern slot frame

Across the twelve providers, the evidence clusters into fifteen practices. Some are proven for one provider and inferred for the rest; the evidence column says which. Read them as the minimum bar a pre-launch studio must clear before a casino aggregator or test lab will take it seriously.

The pattern behind the list matters more than any single row. The shell is deliberately boring so that players moving between a studio's games never relearn controls, while the board is where the studio spends its craft. Regulation has quietly turned the shell into a compliance surface: the very controls that once sold "speed" (turbo, slam stop, autoplay) are now the ones most likely to get a supplier fined.

## 2. Twelve providers, uneven evidence: Pragmatic and Hacksaw are documented, most others are dark

The research team spent its budget on primary sources and discarded a large volume of SEO spam (generated "slot review" pages on hijacked university domains). The result is honest but lopsided. Pragmatic, Hacksaw, Play'n GO and Nolimit City have real, citable evidence in at least one dimension; **BTG, ELK, Thunderkick and Avatar UX have essentially none**, and Push, Relax, NetEnt/Red Tiger and Print Studios have one or two thin facts each. Where a paragraph below says "not verified", it means exactly that.

### Pragmatic Play sets the reference shell

Pragmatic is the best-documented case of a single catalogue-wide shell. The round two-arrow spin button, -/+ bet, an "i" info button at bottom left, a speaker toggle and a three-line settings menu recur across games; the menu also holds total-bet adjustment and Game History, which is unavailable in demo mode ([PlayUSA](https://www.playusa.com/slots/pragmatic-play/sweet-bonanza/); [Clash of Slots](https://clashofslots.com/slots/pragmatic-play/gates-of-olympus/)). Lines games expose coins-per-line (1–10) times coin value (0.01–0.50) inside the bet menu but show total bet on screen, while pays-anywhere games use a single total-bet ladder ([Hot Safari](https://free-slots-no-download.com/pragmatic-play/10450-hot-safari/)). Autoplay runs **10–100 spins** with stop conditions for any win, feature won, single win above a limit, and balance up or down by a set amount, plus Turbo, Quick spin and Skip screens toggles ([Hot Safari](https://free-slots-no-download.com/pragmatic-play/10450-hot-safari/); [PlayUSA](https://www.playusa.com/slots/pragmatic-play/sweet-bonanza/)). Turbo can be held on the space bar on desktop ([Hot Safari](https://free-slots-no-download.com/pragmatic-play/10450-hot-safari/)).

On motion, the verified material is about rules rather than frames. In Gates of Olympus 1000, winning symbols pay, disappear and new symbols tumble down; multiplier orbs (2x–1,000x) land anywhere, and **"at the end of a tumble sequence ... the values of all multiplier symbols on screen are added together and applied to the total win"**; in free spins each multiplier on a winning spin adds to a total that does not reset ([PokerListings](https://www.pokerlistings.com/online-slots/free/gates-of-olympus-1000)). That page lists two different RTPs, so its secondary details deserve caution. On regulation, the info screen shows which RTP variant the operator chose ([Clash of Slots](https://clashofslots.com/slots/pragmatic-play/gates-of-olympus/)). On art, Pragmatic builds franchises around recurring characters: Zeus is Gates of Olympus's "familiar character", and the franchise was the first moved to the POP sub-brand on a portrait 3x5 grid ([FocusGN](https://focusgn.com/pragmatic-play-transforms-its-iconic-zeus-in-gates-of-olympus-pop); [Intergame](https://www.intergameonline.com/igaming/products/gates-of-olympus-pop-launches-new-game-series)). Pragmatic's rendering engine was **not verified**. The landscape layout (spin cluster at right, buy/ante panel at left of the reels, logo top-centre) is unverified recall that must be checked in a live demo.

### Hacksaw builds portrait-first on its own framework

Hacksaw states catalogue-wide that its games are "designed for vertical screens" and include "feature buy, variable bet levels and autoplay", with a 5-icon volatility meter on each game card ([Hacksaw](https://www.hacksawgaming.com/games/slots)). COO Marcus Cordes says the studio "always start[s] designing each game for the smallest screen first"; Stick 'em used "a lower frame rate in the animations to mimic the old cartoon style"; and the first release "introduced a music engine with dynamic progression" ([SlotCatalog](https://slotcatalog.com/en/read/interviews/Marcus-Cordes-COO-at-Hacksaw-Gaming-enters-the-spotlight-for-this-weeks-game-studio-interview)). Engineering ads describe maintaining "our game development framework", accept C#, Haxe, C++, JavaScript and TypeScript, list Pixi only as an example library, and value shader and 3D skills plus "performance, responsiveness, and player experience" ([freehire 2025/26](https://freehire.me/jobs/game-developer-frontend-hacksaw-studios-tgbkzd4j); [freehire 2026](https://freehire.me/jobs/experienced-frontend-gameplay-programmer-hacksaw-studios-rvws7vos)). Art is made in-house in Stockholm with Adobe, After Effects and Spine, and artists hand "reference material that front-end developers use" ([Hacksaw job ad](https://startup.jobs/2d-game-artist-game-development-hacksaw-studios-8236450)). A review site describes the house look as "edgy and bold" with "minimalist yet powerful presentation" and recurring characters (Cranky and Sketchy) across Chaos Crew titles ([Gambling.com](https://www.gambling.com/ie/games/free-slots/hacksaw)). The Haxe hint suggests an engine-agnostic or Haxe-based framework, which is INFERRED.

### Play'n GO owns its engine and treats audio as structure

Play'n GO hires for "our gaming engine", a proprietary front end, with PixiJS, WebGL and Canvas listed as desirable and "user experience, performance and maintainability" stressed ([webbjobb 2022](https://webbjobb.io/jobb/234321/front-end-game-architect)); a 2015 ad shows the Flash-to-HTML5 transition ([webbjobb 2015](https://webbjobb.io/jobb/18147/casino-game-software-developer)). "Most Play n Go slots" offer left-hand mode, a shell-level setting ([SlotsTemple](https://www.slotstemple.com/news-and-blog/the-best-mobile-slots-that-look-great-in-portrait-mode/)). Head of Art & Audio Aleksandra Kuk says "audio is involved from the very beginning", that bonus music escalation is "absolutely crucial", and that sound must stay comfortable over long mobile sessions ([iGaming Expert](https://igamingexpert.com/features/sound-in-slot-development/?amp=1)).

### Nolimit City is proof that identity is tone, not chrome

Nolimit City is documented only on art direction, but well. Per Lindheimer describes pop-culture inspiration, adult targeting and a dislike of "standard (and boring) themes like Egypt and Fruits"; the catalogue has three "legs" (Over the Limit, Classic, Labs "within the studio's style"); sequels can be fully restyled (Tombstone Slaughter); and branded mechanic names (xNudge, xWays, xSplit) act as a catalogue-wide signal ([OLBG](https://www.olbg.com/slots/articles/per-lindheimer)). Editorial coverage describes a palette habit of saturated accents over near-black grounds (Punk Rocker 2, The Crypt) ([MetalTalk](https://www.metaltalk.net/punk-goth-and-metal-nolimit-citys-visual-masterpieces-for-fans-of-dark-aesthetics.php)). Shell, motion and technology were not verified.

### The rest: thin facts and honest gaps

Push Gaming's square grids (Jammin' Jars 8x8) "inherently look good in both portrait and landscape" ([SlotsTemple](https://www.slotstemple.com/news-and-blog/the-best-mobile-slots-that-look-great-in-portrait-mode/)); nothing else was verified. Red Tiger's Thor's Lightning puts the reels across most of the portrait screen with spin and settings along the bottom ([SlotsTemple](https://www.slotstemple.com/news-and-blog/the-best-mobile-slots-that-look-great-in-portrait-mode/)); NetEnt's avalanche multiplier ladder is INFERRED, not verified. Relax's only signal is a recruiter page listing React/JS/cloud tooling with no engine detail ([Toughbyte](https://app.toughbyte.com/companies/relax-gaming-2261)). Print Studios is a Malta studio of "industry veterans" distributed through Relax's Silver Bullet programme, with nothing on its style ([Gaming Intelligence](https://games.gamingintelligence.com/?p=451)). For BTG, ELK, Thunderkick and Avatar UX the team found nothing citable (ELK's art-director post was closed and empty; a SiGMA listing for Avatar UX had no style content) ([Talentadore](https://ats.talentadore.com/apply/art-director/DPkyw2); [SiGMA](https://sigma.world/directory/game-providers/avatarux/)). Familiar observations about these studios (BTG's Megaways frames, Thunderkick's painterly characters) stay unverified and are not used for decisions here.

## 3. The Lumina Shell: an original frame built from Lumina's existing deck

Lumina does not start from zero. Vault 20K already has a single message line (`#psTicker`), value readouts held in a never-rendered `.hud-state`, one notification lifecycle (`VxNotify`) for every popup, hide-in-place market switches (`.is-reg-off`), a reality-check dialog, an embed bridge, and a "zero layout thrash" invariant (repository `CLAUDE.md`). The Lumina Shell spec below extracts those into a shared, game-agnostic package and fills the gaps the research exposed. It follows the industry's functional conventions (spin, bet, info, settings, autoplay, buy) because those are what players expect, but it **does not copy Pragmatic's shapes, icon set, menu layout or button graphics**: Lumina's signature is a single-line ticker instead of a top bar, a deck that never moves the board, and a reserved compliance strip present in every market.

### Design principles (REC)

The shell is DOM; the board is a pluggable canvas view, so a renderer change never touches the shell. No state change may move or resize the board, so every optional element (session HUD, host panic-button zone, feature rail) has reserved space in every market and simply fills or empties it. Turning a market feature off hides the control in place and explains why in Info. Every money figure is shown in currency, never credits, because Ontario requires net position "in Canadian dollars" ([AGCO 2.21](https://agco.ca/en/book/export/html/245361)). Rules never rely on sound alone, which New Jersey requires ([N.J.A.C. 13:69O-1.5(j)(2)](https://www.nj.gov/lps/ge/docs/Regulations/CHAPTER69O.pdf)).

### Component list

| Component | Purpose | Notes (REC unless labelled) |
|---|---|---|

| **Spin button** | Start a round; becomes Stop where the market allows it | Release-then-press required per spin (UK RTS 14D, VERIFIED); no hold-to-spin in UK-style markets |

### Sizes and control states

Phone tap targets never fall below a 44x44 CSS-px hit area; the visual icon can be smaller if padding makes up the difference, and the absolute floor for any secondary control is 40 px with at least 8 px between targets. Numerals for Balance, Bet and Win use one shared tabular face at 16–20 px on phones, and labels never drop below 12 px. These are REC values chosen to meet the requested 40–44 px floor; **no provider published tap-target sizes**, and the research found no evidence that any provider documents accessibility targets, which makes this a genuine differentiation opportunity rather than a catch-up.

### Settings and Info contents

The **Menu** contains Master sound, Music, Sound effects (split, per the VERIFIED Pragmatic convention), Speed (Normal / Quick / Turbo; hidden where banned), Intro screen on/off, Left-hand mode, Reduced motion, Battery saver (caps the board at 30 fps), Language, and a Game history link supplied by the operator. Two consequences deserve emphasis. First, in the UK a bonus buy is a stake, so **the highest base bet at which Buy can be offered equals the stake cap divided by the buy multiple** (at a 100x price, £0.05 for a 25+ player), and the ante-adjusted bet must also clamp under the cap; the cap and the player's age band must come from the RGS or launch, never be hard-coded ([UKGC stake guidance](https://www.gamblingcommission.gov.uk/licensees-and-businesses/guide/online-slots-stake-limit-guidance)). Second, Vault 20K's current win ladder gives a "small" burst and sound to wins below 1x bet (`WIN_TIERS` in `client/main.js`), which is exactly the celebrated sub-stake return that RTS 14F and AGCO 2.20 forbid; the `celebrate_threshold` guard must cover that tier, the ticker's gold WIN state and the per-tumble bursts, judged on the **round** total against the **cycle** stake including ante.

## 4. The Lumina Motion Grammar turns each round into a readable sentence

The grammar has one rule: **the board explains itself in the order the rules apply, and nothing moves that is not explaining something**. Every event below states what the player should understand from it. Timings are **REC values for Lumina**, anchored where possible to Vault 20K's existing constants (columns released 100 ms apart on a spin and 40 ms on a tumble, turbo scale 0.6, impact squash at most 3.5%) and cross-checked against REFERENCE-SPEC ranges, which are shown separately and are not any provider's values. **No row contains a measured provider timing.**

Three column rules apply everywhere. "Turbo" is disabled in UK, Ontario and every profile with `turbo_enabled = false`. "Skippable" means skippable in default markets; in UK-style markets a skip may only shorten presentation **after** the result is communicated, never the time until the result ([UKGC](https://www.gamblingcommission.gov.uk/manual/online-games-design-and-reverse-withdrawals/summary-of-responses-prohibiting-player-led-spin-stop-features)). And the whole cycle never ends before `min_round_ms`; Lumina pads from the RGS answer, not from the button press.

Each game keeps its own tier names (Vault 20K's "VAULT CRACKED / LASER BREACH / MOTHERLODE" already do this). Tiers are evaluated on the round total after the final multiplier, never per tumble, and they are presentation thresholds only: nothing in this table may feed back into the engine or change a payout.

Two further rules make the grammar honest. Anticipation is a pure function of already-landed symbols, so it fires on misses exactly as often as the real probability dictates and never appears when the trigger is already impossible; Ontario explicitly forbids substituting a near-miss loss ([AGCO 2.15](https://agco.ca/en/book/export/html/245361)). And sound reinforces but never carries the message: research found players with win sounds on overestimated how often they won more than they bet by 24% versus 15% without sound ([ScienceDaily on Dixon et al. 2013](https://sciencedaily.com/releases/2013/07/130702100348.htm)), and 13 of 18 hits in a simplified 5-line model were losses disguised as wins ([Harrigan et al. 2011](https://cdspress.ca/wp-content/uploads/2022/08/Kevin-Harrigan-Michael-Dixon-Vance-MacLaren-Karen-Collins-Jonathan-Fugelsang-.pdf)). That second figure is why the 20-line Moth & Lantern needs the no-celebrate rule most of all.

## 5. A rendering library, not an engine: PixiJS on a measured spike, Spine where rigs earn it

The evidence says top providers own their framework and render through WebGL, usually PixiJS. Play'n GO lists PixiJS as desirable for its own engine, Hacksaw names Pixi as an example library inside its own framework, and iGaming job boards ask for "Pixi.js, TypeScript, JavaScript, WebGL" ([webbjobb](https://webbjobb.io/jobb/234321/front-end-game-architect); [freehire](https://freehire.me/jobs/game-developer-frontend-hacksaw-studios-tgbkzd4j); [Djinni, Onlyplay](https://djinni.co/jobs/852081-front-end-game-developer-javascript-pixijs/)). A practitioner calls PixiJS "the industry standard" for slot clients and describes a monorepo of shared, "highly parametrised" components with one entry point per game, and notes that **certified games are not updated afterwards; new features become sequels** ([Medium](https://medium.com/@dragoje.jadranko/organising-a-casino-slot-client-application-de7fb947b1e4)). No primary source showed any top provider using Unity WebGL, Cocos or Construct for real-money slots.

Lumina's position is different from theirs in one important way: its Canvas2D client already implements by hand what Pixi offers as features (a device-tier governor, pooled particles, a sprite cache, a context-loss guard, zero layout shift) and enforces motion rules most studios never write down. So the decision is not "engine or no engine" (**no full engine**: Cocos means a rewrite into a scene/component model, and Unity WebGL first loads are typically several MB, INFERRED), but whether the board view should move to a WebGL library. The recommendation is to **ship Vault 20K on its tested Canvas2D renderer, put every board behind a stable BoardView interface now, and run a one-to-two-week PixiJS v8 spike on Moth & Lantern** with explicit pass/fail gates. Relic Forge's molten-gold and engraving effects are the strongest case for WebGL shaders and filters; Moth & Lantern's rigid cut-paper layers are the cheapest place to prove the port. The GRAVITY/WATERFALL timing maths and the pooling port unchanged because they are renderer-agnostic, and `client/engine/` stays untouched, which preserves RTP parity.

### Asset pipeline and audio

With Pixi, the pipeline is AssetPack building atlases and format variants into a manifest that falls back from KTX2/Basis to WebP/PNG, with `@0.5x` SD tiers chosen by DPR and device tier ([PixiJS docs](https://pixijs.com/8.x/guides/components/assets/compressed-textures.md); [performance tips](https://pixijs.com/8.x/guides/concepts/performance-tips)). ## 6. Three worlds inside one frame, and a lightning bolt that must change

The studio identity follows Nolimit City's structure without its look: one shared tone ("crafted, luminous, artisanal"), one mechanic vocabulary, and one palette habit (a single luminous accent glowing out of a dark or textured ground), with a new world per game ([OLBG](https://www.olbg.com/slots/articles/per-lindheimer)). The shell, the numeral typeface, the ticker and the motion grammar stay identical across all three games; each game owns its frame, symbols, palette, logotype, music, bonus scene change and the visual vocabulary of its signature mechanic. Each game's winning-highlight colour is unique to it and never used at full saturation in its background. Symbols must separate by silhouette alone in a 48 px grayscale render, with hue as a second cue, and specials must differ in kind (label plate, frame shape, motion) rather than only colour (INFERRED rule built on the sourced principle that slot symbols need "exaggerated forms and simplified structure" because "nobody's studying them at leisure", [Inkration](https://inkration.com/the-art-of-game-art-how-casino-and-slot-visuals-are-really-made/)). The background is a "theatrical stage" that must not compete with reels or UI ([Inkration](https://inkration.com/the-art-of-game-art-how-casino-and-slot-visuals-are-really-made/)), which fits Lumina's still-board rule exactly.

### Vault 20K: tumbles and multipliers in a teal-and-gold vault

Vault 20K's world is a deep teal vault mechanism with gold for premiums, specials and wins; lows are teal-family tokens told apart by shape. The frame is machined metal and stone; the bonus scene shift opens the vault doors from teal-dark to a gold-lit interior. The signature animations are the tumble chain (burst, gravity fall, refill) and the multiplier lifecycle: a multiplier lands as a stand-in, is revealed, then at the end of the sequence (or whatever order the engine payload dictates; the presentation must follow the engine, not the other way round) the values travel to a sum badge and one apply-strike multiplies the round total. Ante and buy live in the feature rail, skinned as vault hardware.

**Trade-dress caution.** Vault 20K shares its mechanical family with Pragmatic's Gates of Olympus and Sweet Bonanza (6x5 pays anywhere, tumbles, multipliers, free spins, buy and ante). Mechanics are not trade dress, but the **combination of visual cues** is where risk concentrates. In Gates of Olympus the character Zeus casts the lightning that lands multipliers, and multipliers appear as numbered orbs (INFERRED, unverified observation that must be checked in a demo). Vault 20K's current "STRIKE", in which **lightning strikes the cell and it turns into the multiplier under the flash**, sits uncomfortably close to that signature beat. The recommendation is to keep the timing and the no-tell structure but replace the source and the shape. The reveal should come from inside the vault and travel sideways or from the frame, never from the sky: a lock-bolt arc from the frame rail, a laser-cutter line (matching the existing "LASER BREACH" tier name), or a tumbler dial clicking into a value. The multiplier itself should be a machined hex plate or dial rather than a glowing sphere. No robed sky-god, storm clouds above the grid, or vertical bolt from top of screen. Get a quick IP review of the final key frames before launch; this report is not legal advice.

### Moth & Lantern: twenty lines and a tripling night garden in cut paper

Moth & Lantern's world is a cut-paper night garden: layered paper silhouettes with real drop-shadow depth, hue blocks separating the low symbols, warm lantern light as the only saturated colour, and a paper-vine frame. Its key mechanics are lines, a wild and scatter-triggered free spins where every win is tripled, so its grammar must make three causes legible. **Lines**: each win draws as a paper thread through the winning cells, all lines together first, then one at a time with its amount, a cycle that stops instantly on the next press. **Wild**: the wild stamps onto its cell with a paper-fold flip, and a line it completes shows the wild lit along the thread so the substitution is visible. **x3**: in free spins every line win first shows its base amount, then a lantern ignites over the line and a "x3" tag stamps on, and the amount triples in place before joining the feature total; the player always sees base, multiplier, result. The bonus shift moves from dusk to full night with every lantern lit. Stepped animation (holding drawings on twos, a Hacksaw-documented stylistic technique, not a look) suits paper ([SlotCatalog](https://slotcatalog.com/en/read/interviews/Marcus-Cordes-COO-at-Hacksaw-Gaming-enters-the-spotlight-for-this-weeks-game-studio-interview)), but landings stay at full frame rate so timing reads cleanly. Because 20-line games generate many sub-stake hits ([Harrigan et al. 2011](https://cdspress.ca/wp-content/uploads/2022/08/Kevin-Harrigan-Michael-Dixon-Vance-MacLaren-Karen-Collins-Jonathan-Fugelsang-.pdf)), this game is where the no-celebrate rule must be designed in from the first storyboard.

### Relic Forge: clusters, a meter that remembers, and a relic draft in engraving and molten gold

Relic Forge's world is copperplate engraving on parchment: low symbols as engraved tool and rune glyphs in one ink colour, premiums with molten-gold fill, so gold is the only saturated colour and wins pop by contrast; the frame is an engraved plate with rivets; the bonus shift goes from cold parchment to forge glow. Clusters outline as an engraved burr line around the connected group, so the player sees adjacency as the cause. Each cleared cluster sends a molten charge into the meter **before** the refill falls, so every meter change is attributed to the cluster that caused it. Because the meter persists between sessions, the game must show the meter's current state and source at load (a short "your forge holds N charge" reveal in the intro) and explain persistence in Info; that state must be server-authoritative and restored from the RGS, never from local storage (REC; persistent-state disclosure rules were not researched and must be checked with the test lab). In the relic draft, offers engrave onto plates one by one, the chosen relic is struck in gold, and its effect is demonstrated on the next board. Whether the draft is a real choice that changes expected value or a presentation of a predetermined outcome is a math question the game rules already answer; the animation and Info must match it exactly, and math, RTP and payouts must not change to suit the presentation.

### What to test with real players

Testing must use play money and recruited players who are not paid in winnings, run on real phones, and observed rather than surveyed. The single most valuable test is **cause-and-effect comprehension**: after a round, ask "why did you win that amount?" and "what did the multiplier (or x3, or meter) do?"; the target is that at least 8 of 10 players answer correctly without help after three rounds. Run a **five-second recall test** of each game's key art and logo against two competitor tiles to check identity. Measure **thumb accuracy and reach**: missed taps on spin, bet and Stop in portrait and landscape, and whether left-hand mode gets used. Compare **speed preference** by letting players choose normal or turbo in a default-market build and logging which they keep. Check **sub-stake perception** by showing UK-mode and default-mode builds and asking players to estimate how often they won more than they bet; the Dixon findings predict the default build inflates that estimate ([ScienceDaily](https://sciencedaily.com/releases/2013/07/130702100348.htm)). Finally, test **readability**: grayscale and colour-blind simulations of every symbol at 48 px, and whether players can find the balance, the bet and the session strip in under two seconds.

## Conclusion

The research shifts the question from "how do we look like a top provider?" to "where is the frame becoming a compliance surface, and where is the board still free?". Every control that once signalled premium speed (turbo, slam stop, autoplay, sub-stake fanfare) is now market-gated and enforced against suppliers, so a pre-launch studio that builds those switches, a reserved compliance strip and a celebration gate into its shell from day one is ahead of studios retrofitting them. Lumina's self-imposed rules (a perfectly still board, no shake, a single message line, a renderer that is a pure function of the payload) were written for readability, but they also make UK-mode and reduced-motion builds almost free, and no provider evidence found shows anyone else documenting those as standards.

The weakest part of this report is also the most actionable: not one provider timing was measured. The highest-value next week of work is therefore not art or code but Phase 0, frame-stepping a dozen real demos and logging bundle sizes, because it converts every REC and REFERENCE-SPEC number above into something defensible in front of an operator or a test lab. The one design change that should not wait for that measurement is Vault 20K's lightning strike: it is the single place where Lumina's own game drifts toward a competitor's signature beat, and fixing it now is cheaper than fixing it after a lobby launch.
